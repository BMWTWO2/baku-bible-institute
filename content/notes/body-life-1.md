This session, led by Bruce Wright, focused on the theological foundations of "Body Life," emphasizing the believer's secure standing in grace and the mandate for spiritual restoration over religious performance.

## Core Theological Theses

### The Nature of "Standing" (Romans 5:2)

The Pastor distinguished between a precarious and a secure position in grace. Using a table as an illustration, he noted that believers are not "balls" sitting on top of the table (liable to roll off), but are functionally integrated as the legs of the table. Standing in grace is a fixed, secure state within Christ, rather than a conditional status based on performance.

### Treasure in Earthen Vessels (2 Corinthians 4:6-10)

The group discussed the paradox of divine power revealed through human weakness.

- **The Face of Christ:** Studying Scripture is intended to reveal the "face of Jesus Christ" (a look of acceptance/the groom's gaze) rather than a religious frown.
- **Weakness as Qualification:** Human inability is the primary qualification for the manifestation of God's strength.
- **Spirit-Soul Distinction:** The spirit is deemed perfect before God upon belief, while the soul (emotions, volition, and consciousness) undergoes a progressive conversion to align with that perfection.

### The Definition of "Work" (John 6:28-29)

In response to the question "What shall we do that we might work the works of God?", the Pastor emphasized that the "work" is exclusively to believe on the One whom the Father sent. This rejects the religious impulse to prioritize tithing or attendance as the primary metrics of spiritual health.

## Body Life

Body Life was defined as the reality of fellowship that transcends language, culture, and nationality. It is characterized not by attendance, but by mutual edification and restoration.

### The Restoration Mandate (Galatians 6:1)

The group analyzed the failures of the Corinthian church — specifically their pride ("puffed up" state) regarding a member's sin.

- **The Goal:** Restoration, not condemnation.
- **The Method:** "Restore" is used as a medical term, similar to setting a fractured bone so it can heal.
- **The Requirement:** Only those "walking in the Spirit" are qualified to restore others, and they must do so in a spirit of meekness to avoid the temptation of self-righteous judgment.

### The Restoration Process

- Brother overtaken in fault → Spiritual members identify need
- Spiritual members identify need → Restore in spirit of meekness
- Restore in spirit of meekness → Bone is set (Healing)
- Restore in spirit of meekness → Guard against self-temptation/judgment

### The "Spiritual Limp" (Romans 15:1)

The Pastor introduced the concept of the "spiritual limp." Just as a body does not amputate a sprained ankle but supports it until it heals, the strong in the church are obligated to bear the infirmities of the weak. Body Life is found in looking for those who are struggling and ministering to them, rather than seeking personal gain.
