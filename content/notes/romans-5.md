## Lesson Content Overview

This session established the foundational "Place of Grace," emphasizing that a believer's standing before God is a supernatural work of divine grace rather than a result of human performance or religious achievement.

### The Theology of Standing

- **The Foundation:** Grace provides two distinct benefits: **Access** and **Standing**. Our position in the body of Christ (1 Corinthians 12:12–13) is independent of personal works.
- **Peace with God:** Peace is a work of grace to be received, not earned. It addresses the soul and spirit rather than just the intellect, providing stability during external turbulence.
- **The "Passport" Illustration:** Jesus Christ serves as the believer's passport to God's presence. Access is granted because God the Father sees the image of His Son within the believer, not the believer's personal history, problems, or "goodness."

### Nature vs. Deed

- **Sin Nature:** Humans are classified as sinners by nature (who they are) rather than exclusively by deeds (what they do).
- **Repentance:** Defined as *metanoia* — a change of mind and heart. True repentance is changing how one thinks about God and themselves, prioritizing God's mercy over self-righteousness.

### Scriptural Framework

- **Romans 5:1–5:** The primary text for understanding justification by faith and the resulting access to grace.
- **Luke 18:9–14:** The contrast between the Pharisee (self-trust) and the Publican (mercy-seeker).
- **1 John 1:7–9:** The mechanics of "walking in the light." This involves acknowledging the "sin nature" (singular) and confessing "sins" (plural), which means agreeing with God's assessment of one's condition.

### The Triage of Righteousness

**Basis of Trust:** Where is the individual's confidence placed?

- **Personal performance/works — The Pharisee (Self-Righteous):** Focuses on what they *don't* do wrong and their religious rituals. Blind to the sin nature. Result: No Justification.
- **The mercy of God/Christ's work — The Publican (Grace-Dependent):** Acknowledges the fallen nature and appeals to divine mercy. Result: Justified by Grace.
